Previous methods that nominate phenotype-related subpopulations from single-cell or spatial data typically require pairing the query dataset to one bulk expression matrix with phenotype labels, and they rarely support precomputed multi-cohort meta-z signatures. PhenoMapR provides both routes: users can derive a phenotype signature from a labeled bulk matrix or apply built-in TCGA and PRECOG outcomes meta-z tables. Because those built-in signatures aggregate many cohorts, results obtained with the default references are less dependent on any single bulk study than one-to-one transfer methods.

Across bulk, single-cell, and spatial modalities we show that PhenoMapR recovers established biology and yields testable hypotheses in pancreatic adenocarcinoma, Alzheimer’s disease, and facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy. In PAAD, PRECOG-derived scores highlight malignant ductal populations as relatively adverse, consistent with independent analyses, and spatial mapping shows co-localization of high-scoring compartments. In AD and FSHD, custom bulk-derived signatures stratify independent cohorts and nominate disease-associated cell states. Relative to related tools, PhenoMapR emphasizes continuous scores, low computational cost, and an interactive Shiny workflow; efficiency claims should be read alongside the validation experiments below rather than as a substitute for accuracy.

**Limitations.** Several constraints follow from the scoring model and reference design. First, PhenoMapR computes a linear weighted sum of expression and bulk phenotype z-scores; it does not model gene–gene interactions, pathways, or nonlinear phenotype effects. Second, bulk references are tissue mixtures, so transferred scores can partly reflect lineage abundance or technical factors (library size, cell cycle) rather than within-lineage phenotype state. We therefore recommend within–cell-type contrasts for marker discovery, optional activity-adjusted scoring, and donor-aware sensitivity checks when patient structure is available. Third, the default gene-shuffle null assesses alignment between signature weights and expression, not phenotype label validity, spatial autocorrelation, or patient confounding. Fourth, shipped PRECOG/TCGA matrices are size-compressed by setting |z| ≤ 2 to missing (matching the default cutoff), which limits sensitivity analyses that would require denser upstream tables. Fifth, drug–gene annotations (for example DGIdb) support hypothesis generation only; category labels such as “clinically actionable” are not equivalent to validated therapeutic targets without orthogonal functional evidence. Finally, spatial interpretations that rely on single-cell–to-spot mapping inherit mapping uncertainty and should be confirmed with spot-native scores where possible.

PhenoMapR is intended as a scalable bridge from well-powered bulk phenotype studies to cellular and spatial resolution. Used with appropriate controls for confounding and with independent validation, it can prioritize samples, cells, and locations for deeper experimental follow-up across cancer and non-cancer indications.
